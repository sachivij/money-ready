# Money Ready: review and backlog (1 November 2026)

A holistic review of the app on `main` at commit `d9eab97`, written for Sumeet and Ridhi. It covers viability (users, value, Jump$tart approval, privacy, school computers, maintenance) and ends with a prioritized backlog. Edit the backlog table as items get done.

## Summary

The app is technically sound and further along than most student prototypes: it runs offline, loads nothing external, has no console errors on any page, and already generates a complete, scripted, age-adjusted workshop. The risk is not the code. It is that nobody outside the two builders has used it in a classroom, and Jump$tart has not seen it. Every page says "pending review and approval by Virginia Jump$tart", and the whole value depends on that review happening.

Three things matter most in the next month:

1. Get one real Teen Teach-In volunteer to teach one real lesson from the builder, and watch. That is worth more than any feature.
2. Get a named Jump$tart contact to look at the site and say what they would need to allow a pilot. Until then, keep building content, but stop adding features that only matter at scale (live join, matching).
3. Stop the maintenance load from growing: the repo now has three overlapping game systems, a stale audit script, and a 4-H edition that is drifting. Finish spec 11, then consolidate before adding more.

The backlog below is ordered by that logic: prove use first, then approval, then cleanup, then growth.

## What the app is today, and how it was checked

Money Ready is a static site (plain HTML, CSS and JavaScript, no build step) that turns Virginia Jump$tart Teen Teach-In topics into a scripted, interactive workshop a teen volunteer can run. Five tabs: Resources, Games, Workshop Builder, Volunteer Prep and Standards Helper. The builder is the core: topic, age group, length, group size, class size, setting and devices in, and out comes a timed run of show with word-for-word Say/Do lines, a game with pre-written rounds, printable answer cards, a take-home card and a packet (deck guide, parent letter, checklist, sign-up sheet, reflection).

## Viability

### Who uses it, and what it is worth to them

The primary user is a first-time teen volunteer with a Grade 1-4 class and under 30 minutes to prepare. For that person the builder is genuinely useful: one page gives a script, a game, cards and a plan that adds up to the exact minutes. Nothing comparable exists in the Teen Teach-In resources, which are slide decks and worksheets. That is the product's real edge and it is worth protecting.

Two gaps weaken that value today:

- **The generated plan is long.** The K-2 Needs vs. Wants plan is about 11,600 characters and eight screens tall. A nervous volunteer needs a one-page run sheet to hold in the classroom, with the full packet behind it. Today the detail buries the plan.
- **Module content was not rewritten from the decks.** The Grade 1 module challenge asks a first grader about "$25, a game skin and a promise to save $5". That is a Grade 5 scenario with a Grade 1 label. The official-lesson steps are faithful to the decks; the module cards, challenges and reveals are not. `previous-context.md` already lists this as open.

There is no evidence of use yet. The students-reached sheet (spec 09) went live on 30 October, and nobody has reported a workshop taught from the site. Until one happens, every priority call is a guess.

### Jump$tart approval and licensing

The site uses the Jump$tart name and the Teen Teach-In name on every page, in the title, and in the description, and summarises their five decks slide by slide. The content is in the project's own words with citations and the decks themselves are not in the repo, which is the right posture. But the name use is trademark use, and "Money Ready Virginia" in the page title reads like a Jump$tart product. The "pending review and approval" badges are honest, and they are also the biggest risk: a reviewer who sees their brand on a public site they did not approve may react badly before reading further.

What would make this safe:

1. A named contact at Virginia Jump$tart who has seen the site, in writing. The PRD's open question 6 ("what does Jump$tart need to see before an official pilot?") is still unanswered a month later.
2. One short page in the repo stating what is taken from Jump$tart, how it is used, and that nothing is copied verbatim. Spec 11's `approved_by` field is the right mechanism; a Jump$tart reviewer on `content/` (the CODEOWNERS idea in spec 11) would make it durable.
3. A fallback brand if they say no. The 4-H edition shows the app works without the Jump$tart framing, and the `officialLesson` tag switch already exists.

The trusted-source answers (CFPB, FDIC, FTC, SEC, IRS, NAIC) are U.S. government consumer education and safe to summarise.

### Privacy with students

This is the strongest part of the app. No accounts, no names, no cookies, no third-party scripts, `localStorage` wrapped in try/catch for private browsing, read-aloud uses the browser's own voice, and the one outbound request (the anonymous student count) is documented in code, on the home page and in the modal that asks for it. The count goes to a Google Apps Script web app deployed with "Anyone" access; that is fine for a count, and the sheet itself stays private. Three small things to tighten:

- The take-home card for Needs vs. Wants asks "Pick one family purchase this week." The lesson notes say never to ask students about family finances. Several `familyQuestion` prompts sit on the same line; a wording pass is cheap.
- The module challenge shows a "Room PLAY-XXXX" code that does nothing (there is no join). A volunteer will try to use it. Remove it until spec 10 exists, or label it as decoration.
- The "Students with devices can play along" link on the on-screen game sends children to a URL with `solo=1`; nothing is collected, but the plan should say so in the Say script, since a teacher will ask.

Spec 10 (live join from students' phones) would be the first thing that sends anything from a child's device. The spec is right that it needs Jump$tart and school sign-off and a hosted service. It should wait until there is a pilot that wants it.

### School computers

Spec 03 is not started and nothing has been tested on a Chromebook or a filtered school network. The good news: the site loads nothing from outside itself, so content filters cannot break it, and the only external call fails silently. The likely problems are mundane and untested: print layouts for cards and the take-home card on a school printer, projector readability of the dark challenge screen, `speechSynthesis` voices on managed Chromebooks (often disabled or very robotic), and the `#/` hash routing under some district proxies that rewrite URLs. This is a two-hour test with a borrowed device, and it should happen before any pilot.

### Maintenance with two people

The team is one student owner who works through the GitHub web UI and the live site, plus Ridhi, each driving their own Claude. In practice Claude writes nearly all the code: of the last 40 commits, 21 are authored by Claude, and the rest are merges and spec claims. That works while the specs are clear, and it has produced a lot of code quickly. It also means nobody on the team has the whole app in their head, so the main risk is accumulated complexity nobody can untangle later. Concretely:

- **Three overlapping game systems.** Module challenges in `data.js` (one scenario, A-D answers, timer, fake room code), 21 game formats in `lessons.js` (two with on-screen `play` engines in `play.js`), and builder topic games in `game-bank.js` (rounds per topic, K-2 picture rounds, on-screen vote tally at `#/game`). They were added in three different weeks and do similar things in different ways. PR #15 adds a fourth path (picture challenge) by reusing `game-bank` rounds, which is the right direction.
- **`app.js` and `lessons.js` are both the shared hot spots** the CLAUDE.md warns about, and both keep growing. Every spec touches them.
- **No automated check runs on a PR except the content build.** `scripts/audit.js` is broken by the October tab removals and is not in CI. A green check that means something (every route renders, no console errors, no external requests, plan minutes add up) would catch the regressions two parallel builders will cause.
- **The 4-H edition is derived by string replacement** (`scripts/4h-build/strip.py` and `rebrand.py`) and has not been rebuilt since the games and builder work landed. The longer it waits, the harder the rebuild. Either rebuild it on a schedule or decide it is frozen.
- **The PRD is a month stale.** It still lists the Facilitator dashboard, role picker and impact snapshot as current features, and says GitHub Pages where the site is on Vercel. New builders read it first.

None of this is a crisis. It is the normal state of a prototype that grew fast, and the fix is a short consolidation pass before the next wave of features.
